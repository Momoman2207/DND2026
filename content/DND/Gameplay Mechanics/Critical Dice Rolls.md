Sometimes when rolling a D20, you roll a 1 or a 20. These values are considered to be Critical Hits and Critical failures.

**Critical Hits**
When an unmodified roll of a 20 is rolled on a D20, a critical hit is rolled. (Some abilities allow for critical hits to happen at different values, such as on a 19 and a 20) This represents the check being rolled for doing the best it could have possibly gone. This can be anywhere from a flawless performance to a strengthened blow to an enemy. Critical hits modify the result of the action as follows:

Skill checks or saving throws:
A critical hit on a skill check or saving throw doubles the modified outcome of that dice roll. A die roll of 20 with a modifier of +4 results in a total result of 48. This means that almost always a critical hit is an automatic success. This success does not mean that anything can happen, but merely the best that could happen does.

Attacks:
When rolling to hit an opponent, a critical hit acts to double the total damage of an attack. An attack that does D12 +4 damage would do double the result of that dice and modifier. A roll of 12 results in a damage of 16 doubled by a critical hit to 32 damage. A dice roll of 4 results in a damage of 8 doubled to 16. This often pairs with another rule: If the hit die result is double the AC of the thing being attacked, regardless of if that result was a critical hit, the attack does double damage as described above. This can and often does result in a 4 times damage multiplier.

**Critical Failure**
When an unmodified roll of 1 is rolled on a D20, a critical failure is rolled. (Some abilities allow for critical failures to happen at different values, such as on a 1 and 2) This results in the worst possible thing occurring as a result of that roll. No modifiers can be added or subtracted from a critical failure. This can range from a stealthy mission turning into a vicious brawl to a swung ax hitting yourself or an ally. Critical failures modify the result of the action as follows:

Skill checks:
A critical failure on a skill check results in that event transpiring the worst it could have. A performer getting tomatoes thrown at them, a character trying to recall history remembering false information, and so on.

Saving Throws:
A critical failure on a saving throw means that whatever the save was against gets duplicated in severity. a 10 foot push turns into a 20, saving against 12 damage turns into 24. DM discretion is advised for any and all cases in which the result of this failure is not clear.

Attacks:
A critical failure on a hit roll represents an attacker missing entirely and hitting a friendly creature or character, if not themselves. If there is a friendly creature or character within range of the attack, roll damage against that creature or character. If there are multiple, the DM is to decide which gets hit, giving preference to who would be more likely to get hit in the individual scenario (The DM should favor what is best for the players when deciding who to hit if no clear choice arises). If no creatures or characters are within range of the attacker, the attacker resolves the hit against themselves.

**Critical Failures: Magic**
Some rolls on [[The magic table]] result in a critical failure, these are known as miss fires. This can lead to varied results. If the spell does not clearly belong to one of these categories, the DM may place it in a category they believe fits best.

Utility spell:
If the spell is meant for utility, such as a invisibility spell or a movement spell, treat it like a critical failure on a skill check and have the DM (or the player) come up with a fitting way to describe how the spell miss fires. This is often what is the opposite effect of the spell or a involuntary use case of the spell that is undesirable.

Healing spell:
If the spell is meant as a spell to either heal, stabilize, or resurrect a creature or character, a miss fire of the spell will result in the opposite occurring. This can look like a spell damaging instead of healing, permanently killing instead of resurrecting, or killing instead of stabilizing etc.

Direct damage spell:
A spell that's entire focus is on doing damage, such as fireball or magic missile, that miss fire results in that damage being applied to another location or target than originally intended. This is chosen by the DM, although player input is allowed based on the scenario. The DM must use what is the most likely outcome as the new target, unless no obvious new targets are available (Don't be really mean about it).

Damaging physical spell:
A miss fire of a spell that interacts with the environment in any way, usually in the form of placing down a hazard on the map such as a wall of fire, results in that physical spell being cast in a different location than initially intended. The DM takes over placing the obstacle, choosing to either center the spell on the caster or in a separate location that would make sense given the scenario. The DM must place it the most likely location for it to land, and failing to locate an obvious location, may center it on the caster.
