[[The All-Father]] is worshiped as a near unlimited number of gods, however the most common gods worshiped in the place of [[The All-Father]] are the many gods that make up the Greek Pantheon. These include:

**Male Gods**

#### [Achelous](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/achelous/)

The patron god of the “silver-swirling” Achelous River.  
  

#### [Aeolus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/aeolus/)

Greek god of the winds and air  
  

#### [Aether](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/aether/)

Primordial god of the upper air, light, the atmosphere, space and heaven.  
  

#### [Alastor](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/alastor/)

God of family feuds and avenger of evil deeds.  
  

#### [Apollo](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/apollo/ "Apollo")

Olympian god of music, poetry, art, oracles, archery, plague, medicine, sun, light and knowledge.  
  

#### [Ares](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/ares/ "Ares")

God of war. Represented the physical, violent and untamed aspect of war.  
  

#### [Aristaeus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/aristaeus/)

Minor patron god of animal husbandry, bee-keeping, and fruit trees. Son of [Apollo](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/apollo/).  
  

#### [Asclepius](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/asclepius/)

God of medicine, health, healing, rejuvenation and physicians.  
  

#### [Atlas](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/atlas/)

The Primordial Titan of Astronomy. Condemned by Zeus to carry the world on his back after the Titans lost the war.  
  

#### [Attis](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/attis/)

A minor god of vegetation, fruits of the earth and rebirth.  
  

#### [Boreas](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/boreas/)

A wind god (Anemoi) and Greek god of the cold north wind and the bringer of winter. Referred to as “The North Wind”.  
  

#### [Caerus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/caerus/)

Minor god of opportunity, luck and favorable moments.  
  

#### [Castor](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/castor/)

One of the twins, [Castor and Pollux](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/heroes/dioscuri/), known as Dioscuri. Zeus transformed them into the constellation Gemini.  
  

#### [Cerus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/cerus/)

The large and powerful wild bull tamed by [Persephone](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/persephone/) and turned into the Taurus constellation.  
  

#### [Chaos](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/chaos/)

The nothingness that all else sprung from. A god who filled the gap between Heaven and Earth and created the first beings Gaia, Tartarus, Uranus, Nyx and Erebus.  
  

#### [Charon](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/charon/)

The Ferryman of Hades. Took the newly dead people across the rivers Styx and Acheron to the Greek underworld if they paid him three obolus (a Greek silver coin).  
  

#### [Cronos](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/cronus/)

Greek name: Κρόνος (_Krónos_)

The god of time. Not to be confused with Cronus, the Titan father of Zeus.  
  

#### [Crios](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/crios/)

Greek name: Κρεῖος (_Kreîos_)

One of the twelve Titan Gods, Crios was known as the god of the heavenly constellations and the measure of the year. He was father to Astraeus, Pallas, and Perses.  
  

#### [Dinlas](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/dinlas/)

Guardian god of the ancient city Lamark, where wounded heroes could find comfort and heal after battle. He was the son of [Aphrodite](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/aphrodite/).  
  

#### [Deimos](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/deimos/)

Deimos is the personification of dread and terror.  
  

#### [Dionysus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/dionysus/ "Dionysus")

Greek name: Διόνυσος, _Diónusos_

An Olympian god of the grape harvest, winemaking and wine, of ritual madness, religious ecstasy and theatre.  
  

#### [Epimetheus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/epimetheus/)

Greek name: Ἐπιμηθεύς (_Epimētheús_)

God of the afterthought.

  
  

#### [Erebus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/erebus/)

Primordial god of darkness.  
  

#### [Eros](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/eros/)

God of sexual desire, attraction, love and procreation.  
  

#### [Eurus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/eurus/)

One of the wind god known as Anemoi and god of the unlucky east wind. Referred to as “The East Wind”.  
  

#### [Glaucus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/glaucus/)

A fisherman who became immortal upon eating a magical herb, an Argonaut who may have built and piloted the Argo, and became a [god of the sea](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/list-of-ancient-greek-sea-gods/ "god of the sea").  
  

#### [Hades](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/hades/ "Hades")

Greek name: ᾍδης, _Háidēs_

God of the Dead and Riches and King of the Underworld.  
  

#### [Helios](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/helios/)

Greek name: Ἥλιος (_Hḗlios_)

God of the Sun and also known as Sol.  
  

#### [Hephaestus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/hephaestus/ "Hephaestus")

Greek name: Ἥφαιστος, _Hḗphaistos_

God of fire, metalworking, stone masonry, forges and the art of sculpture. Created weapons for the gods and married to Aphrodite.  
  

#### [Heracles](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/heroes/heracles/)

The greatest of the [Greek heroes](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/heroes/), he became god of heroes, sports, athletes, health, agriculture, fertility, trade, oracles and divine protector of mankind. Known as the strongest man on Earth.  
  

#### [Hermes](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/hermes/ "Hermes")

Greek name: Ἑρμῆς, _Hērmês_

God of trade, thieves, travelers, sports, athletes, and border crossings, guide to the Underworld and messenger of the gods.  
  

#### [Hesperus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/hesperus/)

The Evening Star – the planet [Venus](http://theplanets.org/venus/) in the evening.  
  

#### [Hymenaios](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/hymenaios/)

God of marriage ceremonies, inspiring feasts and song.  
  

#### [Hyperion](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/hyperion/)

Greek name: Ὑπερίων (_Hyperíōn_)

Titan God of Heavenly Light & Pillar of the East. Hyperion is the father of Helios, the embodiment of the sun; Selene, the personification of the moon; and Eos, the herald of dawn. His wife, Theia, who belongs to the Titan lineage, shares a familial bond as his sister and presides as the goddess overseeing sight and the azure expanse of the sky.  
  

#### [Hypnos](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/hypnos/)

The Greek god of sleep.  
  

#### [Iapetus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/iapetus/)

Greek name: Ἰαπετός (_Iapetós_)

Iapetus was the Greek god of Mortality. He is the son of Uranus and Gaia.

  
  

#### [Kratos](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/kratos/)

God of strength and power.

  

#### [Lelantos](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/lelantos/)

Greek name: Λήλαντος (_Lēlantos_)

God of Air

  

#### [Menoetius](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/menoetius/)

Greek name: Μενοίτιος (_Menoítios_)

Menoetius is the Titan god of rash actions, violent anger, and human mortality.

####   
  

#### [Momus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/momus/)

God of satire, mockery, censure, writers and poets and a spirit of evil-spirited blame and unfair criticism.  
  

#### [Morpheus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/morpheus/)

God of dreams and sleep – has the ability to take any human form and appear in dreams.  
  

[**Moros**](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/moros/)

Greek name: Μόρος

The physical embodiment of doom  
  

#### [Nereus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/nereus/)

Greek name: Νηρέας

The Titan god of the sea before Poseidon and father of the Nereids (nymphs of the sea).  
  

#### [Notus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/notus/)

Greek name: Νότος

Another Anemoi (wind god) and Greek god of the south wind. Known as “The South Wind”.  
  

#### [Oceanus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/oceanus/)

Greek name: Ὠκεανός (_Ōceanós_)

Titan god of the ocean. Believed to be the personification of the World Ocean, an enormous river encircling the world.  
  

#### [Oneiroi](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/oneiroi/)

Black-winged daimons that personified dreams.  
  

#### [Paean](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/paean/)

Greek name: Παιάν

The physician of the Olympian gods.  
  

#### [Pallas](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/pallas/)

Greek name: Πάλλας (_Pállas_)

The [Titan](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/titans/) god of warcraft and of the springtime campaign season.  
  

#### [Pan](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/pan/)

God of nature, the wild, shepherds, flocks, goats, mountain wilds, and is often associated with sexuality. Also a satyr (half man, half-goat).

#### [Perses](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/perses/)

Greek name: Πέρσης (_Pérsēs_)

The greek god of destruction.  
  

#### [Phosphorus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/phosphorus/)

The Morning Star – [the planet Venus](https://theplanets.org/venus/) as it appears in the morning.  
  

#### [Plutus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/plutus/)

The Greek god of wealth.  
  

#### [Pollux](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/pollux/)

Twin brother of Castor, together known as the Dioskouri, that were transformed into the constellation Gemini.  
  

#### [Pontus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/pontus/)

Greek name: Πόντος (Póntos)

Ancient, pre-Olympian sea-god of the deep sea, one of the Greek primordial deities and son of Gaia.  
  

#### [Poseidon](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/poseidon/ "Poseidon")

Greek name: Ποσειδῶν, _Poseidôn_

Olympian Greek god of the sea, earthquakes, storms, and horses.  
  

#### [Priapus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/priapus/)

Greek name: Πρίαπος

Minor rustic fertility god, protector of flocks, fruit plants, bees and gardens.  
  

#### [Pricus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/pricus/)

The immortal father of sea-goats, made into the Capricorn constellation.  
  

#### [Prometheus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/prometheus/)

Greek name: Προμηθεύς (_Promētheús_)

Titan god of forethought and crafty counsel who was given the task of moulding mankind out of clay.  
  

#### [Primordial](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/primordial/)

A group of gods that came before all else.  
  

#### Syceus

Greek name: Συκεύς (_Sykeús_)

Syceus, a Titan born of Gaia, found refuge from Zeus’s wrath when Gaia transformed him into a fig tree, shielding him from harm.

####   

#### [Tartarus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/tartarus/)

Greek name: Τάρταρος (_Tártaros_)

The god of the deep abyss, a great pit in the depths of the underworld, and father of Typhon.  
  

#### [Thanatos](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/thanatos/)

A minor god and the god of death.  
  

#### [Triton](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/triton/)

Messenger of the sea and the son of Poseidon and [Amphitrite](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/amphitrite/).  
  
  

#### [Uranus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/uranus/)

Primordial [god of the sky](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/sky-gods/) and heavens, and father of the Titans.  
  

#### [Zelus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/zelus/)

The god of dedication, emulation, eager rivalry, envy, jealousy, and zeal.  
  

#### [Zephyrus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/zephyrus/)

A wind god (Anemoi). God of the west wind and known as “The West Wind”.  
  

#### [Zeus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/zeus/ "Zeus")

God of the sky, lightning, thunder, law, order, justice, King of the Gods and the “Father of Gods and men”.










**Female Goddesses**

#### [Achelois](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/achelois/)

A minor moon goddess whose name means “she who washes away pain”.  
  

#### [Alcyone](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/alcyone/)

One of the seven, Pleiades and daughter of Atlas and Pleione. She bore several children with the god [Poseidon](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/poseidon/).  
  

#### [Alectrona](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/alectrona/)

An early Greek goddess of the sun, daughter of Helios and Rhode, and possibly goddess of the morning.  
  

#### [Amphitrite](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/amphitrite/)

Greek goddess of the sea, wife of Poseidon and a Nereid.  
  

#### [Antheia](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/antheia/)

Goddess of gardens, flowers, swamps, and marshes.  
  

#### [Apate](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/apate/)

Goddess of gardens, flowers, swamps, and marshes.  
  

#### [Aphaea](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/aphaea/)

A Greek goddess who was worshipped almost exclusively at a single sanctuary on the island of Aegina in the Saronic Gulf.  
  

#### [Aphrodite](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/aphrodite/ "Aphrodite")

Goddess of love and beauty and married to [Hephaestus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/hephaestus/).  
  

#### [Artemis](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/artemis/ "Artemis")

Virginal goddess of the hunt and twin sister of [Apollo](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/apollo/).  
  

#### [Astraea](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/astraea/)

Known as the “Star Maiden”, daughter of either Zeus and Themis, or of Astraeus and [Eos](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/eos/) and associated with the Greek goddess of justice, Dike.  
  

#### [Até](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/ate/)

Greek goddess of mischief, delusion, ruin, and folly.  
  

#### [Athena](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/athena/ "Athena")

Goddess of wisdom, poetry, art, and war strategy. [Daughter of Zeus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/daughters-of-zeus/) and born from his forehead fully grown, wearing battle armour.  
  

#### [Atropos](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/atropos/)

Eldest of the three Moirai, goddesses of fate and destiny (also known as [The Fates](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/the-fates/)). Atropos chose the mechanism of death and ended the life of each mortal by cutting their thread.  
  

#### [Bia](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/bia/)

The goddess of force and raw energy, daughter of Pallas and Styx, and sister of Nike, Kratos, and [Zelus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/zelus/).  
  

#### [Brizo](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/brizo/)

Ancient Greek prophet goddess who was known as the protector of mariners, sailors, and fishermen.  
  

#### [Calliope](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/calliope/)

One of the Muses, the muse of epic poetry, daughter of Zeus and Mnemosyne and the wisest of the Muses.  
  

#### [Calypso](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/calypso/)

Sea nymph who lived on the island of Ogygia, where she detained [Odysseus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/heroes/odysseus/) for several years. Generally said to be the daughter of the Titan [Atlas](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/atlas/).  
  

#### [Celaeno](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/celaeno/)

One of the Pleiades, and a wife of Poseidon. Said to be the mother of the [sea god’s](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/list-of-ancient-greek-sea-gods/) children Lycus and Nycteus.  
  

#### [Ceto](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/ceto-goddess-of-the-water/)

Primordial sea monster goddess, the daughter of Gaia and [Pontus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/pontus/ "Pontus") and mother of sea monsters.  
  

#### [Circe](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/circe/)

A goddess of magic who transformed her enemies, or those that insulted her, into beasts.  
  

#### [Clio](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/clio-greek-goddess-of-history/)

The muse of history and one of the nine muses known as “The Muses”. Like all the muses, Clio is the daughter of [Zeus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/zeus/) and the Titaness Mnemosyne.  
  

#### [Clotho](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/clotho/)

Youngest of the Three Fates and responsible for spinning the thread of human life.  
  

#### [Cybele](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/cybele/)

The Greek goddess of caverns, mountains, nature and wild animals.  
  

#### [Demeter](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/demeter/ "Demeter")

Goddess of agriculture, fertility, sacred law and the harvest.  
  

#### [Doris](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/doris/)

A sea nymph whose name represented the bounty of the sea. Mother of the Nereids.  
  

#### [Eileithyia](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/eileithyia/)

Goddess of childbirth, referred to by Homer as “the goddess of the pains of birth”.  
  

#### [Electra](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/electra/)

One of the seven daughters of Atlas and Pleione, known as The Pleiades.  
  

#### [Elpis](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/elpis/)

The spirit and personification of hope. Hope was usually seen as an extension to suffering by the Greek, not as a god.  
  

#### [Enyo](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/enyo/)

Minor goddess of war and destruction, the companion and lover of the war god [Ares](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/ares/ "Ares") and connected to Eris.  
  

#### [Eos](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/eos/)

A Titaness and the goddess of the dawn.  
  

#### [Erato](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/erato-muse-of-love-poetry/)

One of the Muses, the muse of lyric poetry, especially love and erotic poetry.  
  

#### [Eris](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/eris/)

Greek goddess of chaos, strife and discord and connected to the war-goddess Enyo.  
  

#### [Euterpe](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/euterpe/)

One of the Muses, the muse of music and lyric poetry.  
  

#### [Gaia](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/gaea/)

The primal Greek goddess of the Earth. Known as the great mother of all and often referred to as “Mother Earth”.  
  

#### [Harmonia](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/harmonia/)

The Greek goddess of harmony and concord.  
  

#### [Hebe](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/hebe/)

Goddess of eternal youth.  
  

#### [Hecate](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/hecate/)

The goddess of magic, crossroads, moon, ghosts, witchcraft and necromancy (the undead).  
  

#### [Hemera](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/hemera/)

Primordial goddess of the day, daytime and daylight. Daughter to Erebus and Nyx (the goddess of night).  
  

#### [Hera](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/hera/ "Hera")

Goddess of goddesses, women, and marriage. Married to Zeus and known as Queen of the Gods.  
  

#### [Hestia](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/hestia/ "Hestia")

goddess of the hearth, home, architecture, domesticity, family, and the state. Also one of the Hesperides.  
  

#### [Hygea](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/hygea-goddess-of-hygiene/)

Goddess of good health, cleanliness, and sanitation. This is where the word “hygiene” comes from.  
  

#### [Iris](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/iris/)

Greek goddess of the rainbow and messenger of the gods. She is also known as one of the goddesses of the sea and the sky.  
  

#### [Keres](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/keres/)

The Keres were female spirits, the daughters of Nyx, the goddess of night.  
  

#### [Kotys](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/kotys/)

A Dionysian goddess whose celebrations were wild and lascivious.  
  

#### [Lachesis](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/lachesis/)

Second of the Three Fates, the measurer of the thread of life woven by Clotho’s spindle which determines Destiny.  
  

#### [Maia](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/maia/)

Eldest of the seven Pleiades and the greek goddess of fields.  
  

#### [Mania](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/mania/)

Spirit goddess of insanity, madness, crazed frenzy and the dead.  
  

#### [Melpomene](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/melpomene/)

One of the Muses. Originally the muse of singing, she then became the muse of tragedy.  
  

#### [Merope](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/merope/)

One of the seven Pleiades and married to king Sisyphos of Ephyra.  
  

#### [Metis](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/metis/)

Titan goddess of wisdom, an [Oceanid](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/oceanids/), and the first great spouse of Zeus.  
  

#### [Nemesis](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/nemesis/)

The goddess of retribution and personification of vengeance.  
  

#### [Nike](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/nike/)

Goddess of victory, known as the Winged Goddess of Victory.  
  

#### [Nyx](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/nyx/)

Primordial goddess of the night.  
  

#### [Peitho](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/peitho/)

Greek goddess of persuasion and seduction.  
  

#### [Persephone](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/persephone/)

Goddess of vegetation and spring and queen of the underworld. Lives off-season in the underworld as the wife of [Hades](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/hades/).  
  

#### [Pheme](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/pheme/)

The goddess of fame, gossip and renown. Her favour is notability, and her wrath is scandalous rumors.  
  

#### [Polyhymnia](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/polyhymnia/)

One of The Muses, the muse of sacred poetry, sacred hymn, dance, and eloquence as well as agriculture, geometry and pantomime.  
  

#### [Rhea](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/rhea/)

Titaness and goddess of nature. Daughter of the earth goddess Gaia and the sky god [Uranus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/uranus/), and known as “the mother of gods”.  
  

#### [Selene](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/selene/)

Goddess of the Moon, sometimes referred to as Luna and the ‘mother’ of vampires.  
  

#### [Sterope](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/sterope/)

One of the seven Pleiades (the daughters of Atlas and Pleione) and the wife of Oenomaus – although according to some accounts, she is his mother by Ares.  
  

#### [Styx](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/styx/)

Goddess of the river Styx and a Naiad who was the first to aid Zeus in the Titan war.  
  

#### [Taygete](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/taygete/)

A mountain nymph and one of the seven Pleiades.  
  

#### [Terpsichore](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/terpsichore/)

Goddess of dance and chorus and one of the nine Muses.  
  

#### [Thalia](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/thalia/)

One of the Muses, the muse of comedy and idyllic poetry.  
  

#### [The Erinnyes](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/the-erinyes/)

#### [The Graces](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/the-graces/)

Goddesses of retribution and vengeance whose job was to punish men who committed heinous crimes.  
  

#### [The Muses](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/the-muses/)

The nine Muses were the goddesses the arts.  
  

#### [Themis](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/themis/)

Ancient Greek Titaness and goddess of divine order, law, natural law and custom.  
  

#### [Thetis](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/thetis/)

Sea nymph, goddess of water and one of the fifty Nereids, daughters of the ancient sea god [Nereus](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/gods/nereus/). Also a shapeshifter and a prophet.  
  

#### [Tyche](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/tyche/)

Goddess of prosperity and fortune.  
  

#### [Urania](https://greekgodsandgoddesses.net/goddesses/urania/)

One of the Muses, the muse of astronomy and astrology.
